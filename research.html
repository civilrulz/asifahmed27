Research Goal

My research experience can be attributed to Geotechnical and Geoenvironmental Engineering which can be summarized as: (1) Performance Monitoring of Geo-infrastructure through Field Instrumentation and FEM Analysis, (2) Geo-physical Testing for Evaluation of Geohazard Potential and Infrastructure Health Monitoring, (3) Laboratory Testing (Both Saturated and Unsaturated) of GeoMaterials, and (4) Non Destructive Testing of Alternative Recycled Materials for Pavement.

During my Geotechnical research, I had the opportunity to work on several projects focused on highway slope stability, pavement distress, subgrade moisture modeling, recycled materials (RAP) testing, geo-physical testing on subbase, use of geosynthetics in pavement moisture control. I was also involved in Bio-reactor Landfills to evaluate the moisture variation and efficiency of leachate recirculation in the Geoenvironmental sector.

My ultimate focus is to maximize the lifespan of the transportation infrastructure through proper investigation, providing cost effective and sustainable solutions.

Research Highlights

Determining the Freeze-Thaw Cycle of Pavement Subgrade

In northern parts of the US, freezing of subgrade soils and subsequent thawing action causes damage to transportation infrastructure. This research aims to identify the actual timing of freezing and thawing based on real-time data.

Sustainable Highway Slope Stabilization using Recycled Plastic Pins (RPP)

Recycled Plastic Pins (RPPs) have been found to be a very cost-effective solution for improving highway slope stability compared to alternative methods.

Pavement Instrumentation to Study the Behavior of Highway Subgrade

Instrumentation provides a continuous dynamic image of the subsurface to observe changes in moisture and temperature on a real-time basis.

Mitigation of Flexible Pavement Crack using Geosynthetic Modified Moisture Barrier

A combination of geotextile, geomembrane, and geonet has been successfully applied to mitigate pavement edge cracking.

Finite Element Modeling (FEM) of Geo-infrastructures

Finite Element Modeling (FEM) is an effective tool to evaluate the performance of highways, pavements, dams, and tunnels under various loading conditions.

Geophysical Testing for Forensic Investigation

Resistivity imaging provides a continuous portrayal of the subsurface and is an efficient tool for failure investigation of highway slopes and seepage through dams.

Use of Recycled Materials in Pavement Base

Rapid depletion of virgin aggregates has increased the use of recycled materials such as Recycled Asphalt Pavement (RAP) and Recycled Crushed Concrete Aggregate (RCCA) in pavement applications.
